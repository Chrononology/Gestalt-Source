**Units** are important in [[Chemistry]] and [[Physics]].

Units are either from the SI System/Metric (_Système international d'unités_) or the Imperial/American System or the obscure metric variant: the CGS system (centimetre-gram-second).

Sciences usually focus more on the standardized SI system.

## Length

The metre/meter (m) is defined as the distance light travels in a vacuum in ${}\frac{1}{299792458}{}$ of a second.

## Mass

The kilogram (kg) is originally based on a reference object (${}1{}$ litre of pure water then platinum-iridium alloy with height and diametre of 39mm). It is now based on several Physical constants.

## Temperature

The kelvin (K) is standard unit of temperature as used by the IUPAC (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry). It is just "K", no degrees. It is written as uppercase as a symbol and all lowercases as a word.

$$
1\;\text{K}=273.15\;^{\circ}\text{C}
$$

## Time

Second (s) is the standard unit of time.

## Volume

The litre/liter (L) is a unit of occupied space by an object. Volume is based on the length of an object cubed. Litre is based on ${}10\;\text{cm}^{3}{}$ or ${}1\;\text{dm}^{3}{}$. Cubic centimetre or "cc" is a term used in the medical field.

## Density

Densitry is the ratio of a substance's mass to its volume. The SI unit is ${}\frac{kg}{m^{3}}{}$ however, ${}\frac{g}{cm^{3}}{}$ is more convenient for solids (and sometimes liquids) and ${}\frac{g}{L}{}$ for gasses
