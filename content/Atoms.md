# Atomic Structure

Atoms are primarily made of three subatomic particles namely: proton, electron, and neutron.

Here are the properties of these subatomic particles:

| Sub-atomic Particle |      Location       |           Mass (g)           |          Charge (C)          | Unit Charge |
| :-----------------: | :-----------------: | :--------------------------: | :--------------------------: | :---------: |
|      Electron       | Outside the Nucleus | ${}9.10938\times 10^{-28}{}$ | ${}-1.6022\times 10^{-19}{}$ |  ${}-1{}$   |
|       Proton        |       Nucleus       | ${}1.67262\times 10^{-24}{}$ | ${}+1.6022\times 10^{-19}{}$ |  ${}+1{}$   |
|       Neutron       |       Nucleus       | ${}1.67493\times 10^{-24}{}$ |           ${}0{}$            |   ${}0{}$   |

## Chemical Notation

A chemical notation (also known as nuclear notation) is usually used to specify what kind of element we are talking about.

$$
^{A}_{Z}X^{C}
$$

Where:
${}X={}$The element symbol
${}A={}$The Mass Number
${}Z={}$The Atomic Number
${}C={}$the Charge of the atom

Related equations:
${}A=p^{+}+n^{0}{}$
${}Z=p^{+}=A-n^{0}{}$
${}C=p^{+}-e^{-}{}$

# Molecules

Molecules are the result of two-or more atoms held or "bonded" together chemically.

## Diatomic Molecules

Diatomic molecules are comprised of only 2 atoms.

$$
H_{2},N_{2},O_{2},F_{2},Cl_{2},Br_{2},I_{2}, HBr
$$

## Polyatomic Molecules

Polyatomic molecules are comprised of 3 or more atoms.

$$
H_{2}O,CO_{2},C_{6}H_{12}O_{6}
$$

# Ions

Atoms are ionized when they gain or lose electrons. When atoms **lose** electrons, they become positively charged and are called **cations**. When atoms **gain** electrons, they become negatively charged and are called **anions**.

## Monoatomic Ions

Monoatomic ions are ions that contain a singular atom

$$
Li^{+},Na^{+},K^{+},Rb^{+},Cs ^{+}
$$

## Polyatomic Ions

Polyatomic ions are ions that contain multiple atoms

$$
NH_{4}^{+},OH^{-},NO_{3}^{-},SO_{4}^{2-}
$$

# Isotopes

Atoms of the same element with different mass numbers are called isotopes.
${}^{16}_{8}O, ^{17}_{8}O, ^{18}_{8}O{}$ are isotopes of each other.

# Compounds and Basic Nomenclature

_Main notes: [[Chemical Nomenclature]]_

Compounds are either bonded together **Ionically** or **Covalently**.
Bonds form because of the atom's desire to be stable by having 8 valence electrons.

## Ionic Bonding

Ionic bonding happens between ions (cations and anions) typically of metal to non-metal natures. This is where the cation (metal) gives its valence electrons to the anion (non-metal). The resulting total charge of the compound must be ${}0{}$.

A normal ionic bonding nomenclature has a formula of:
cation + (anion + -ide)

### Binary Ionic

Binary compounds are compounds with only 2 elements.

Examples:

- Lithium flouride (${}LiF{}$)
- Sodium oxide (${}Na_{2}O{}$)
- Aluminium oxide (${}Al_{2}O_{3}{}$)

### Variable Ionic

Variable ionic compounds are compounds with a variably charged cation. In this case, the charge must be specified with a Roman Numeral

For its nomenclature, we attach the cation's charge as a roman numeral in parenthesis directly after the cation.
cation + (Roman Numeral) + anion

Examples:

- Iridium (III) sulfide (${}Ir_{2}S_{3}{}$)
- Chromium (VI) oxide (${}CrO_{3}{}$)

### Polyatomic Ionic

Polyatomic ionic compounds are compounds with more than two elements. It contains polyatomic ions.

For its nomenclature, we use the whole name of the polyatomic ion.
Examples:

- Caesium hydroxide (${}CsOH{}$)
- Calcium sulfate (${}CaSO_{4}{}$)
- Ammonium nitrate (${}NH_{4}NO_{3}{}$)

## Covalent Bonding

Covalent bonding usually happens between non-metals. This is where non-metals share valence electrons with each other.

A normal covalent bonding nomenclature has a formula of:
(Greek prefix-) + non-metal + (greek prefix-) + non-metal + (-ide)

Usually the Greek prefix of the first non-metal is dropped if: it is only 1 atom.

List of Greek Prefixes:

| Number of Atoms | Prefix |
| :-------------: | :----: |
|        1        | mono-  |
|        2        |  di-   |
|        3        |  tri-  |
|        4        | tetra- |
|        5        | penta- |
|        6        | hexa-  |
|        7        | hepta- |
|        8        | octa-  |
|        9        | nona-  |
|       10        | deca-  |

Examples:

- Nitrogen dioxide (${}NO_{2}{}$)
- Carbon monoxide (${}CO{}$)
- Disulfure pentoxide (${}S_{2}O_{5}{}$)

### Hydrogen + Non-metal

Hydrogen acting as a cation and a non-metal acting as an anion bonding together is a special case.

If the compound is a pure substance its nomenclature is:
Hydrogen + non-metal + -ide

If the compound is dissolved in water or is an aqueous mixture:
hydro- + non-metal + -ic + acid

Examples:

- Hydrogen fluoride
- Hydrofluoric acid
