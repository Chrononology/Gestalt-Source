A **Chemical Formula** is a way of writing the components of a compound using numbers and element symbols with exact number of [[atoms]] per element. Chemical formulas are used in [[Chemistry]] and are usually taught in [[General Chemistry]].

# Types of Chemical Formula

## Molecular Formula

The molecular formula of a compound contains the exact number of atoms per element.
Example:

$$
\ce{C_{6}H_{12}O_{6}}\\
\text{Glucose} 
$$

## Empirical Formula

The empirical formula of a compound is the simplest _whole_ number ratio of atoms of the compound
Example:

$$
\ce{CH_{2}O}\\
\text{Glucose} 
$$

## Structural Formula

The structural formula of a compound is usually used in organic compounds where the structure is drawn.

# Converting Between Chemical Formulas

## Percent Composition to Empirical Formula

Given a percent composition (${}41.2\%X_{1},19.6\%X_{2},39.2\%X_{3}{}$) of a compound...

1. Take each percent then treat it as ${}100g{}$ (${}41.2\%=41.2g{}$)
2. Get the number of moles of each component by dividing the given grams to each component's molar mass
3. Turn the moles into whole numbers by dividing the moles by the smallest one (${}0.21mol, 0.43mol, 0.32mol{}$ divide all by ${}0.21mol{}$)
4. Combine them all as a single formula

## Empirical to Molecular Formula

Given the molar mass of a compound...

1. Take the molar mass of the compound in its empirical form
2. Divide the molar mass of the molecular form by the molar mass of the empirical form
3. Multiply each subscript of the empirical formula by the quotient of Step 2

## Molecular or Empirical Formula to Percent Composition

1. Take the molar mass of the compound and the molar mass of each of its components.
2. Divide each molar mass of its components by the molar mass of the compound
3. Multiply each result by 100
