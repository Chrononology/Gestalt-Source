A **Force** is the interaction of action and objects. It is a central idea of [[Dynamics]]

# Normal Force

The normal force is always _perpendicular_ to the _surface_ and is always applied to an object in contact with a surface.

# Friction Force

The friction force is the force applied to the object by the surface in the opposite direction of the (sliding) motion. It is always parallel to the surface.
This type of force is dependent on the surface and the object's normal force.

## Static Friction

The static friction of an object is the (relative) amount of force needed to move an object (from rest). It is represented by this equation:

$$
f_{s}\leq\mu_{s} n
$$

## Kinetic Friction

The kinetic friction of an object is the (relative) amount of force needed to keep the object moving (at a constant speed). The kinetic friction of an object is directly proportional to its normal force. It is represented by this equation:

$$
f_{k}=\mu_{k}n
$$

## Friction on an Incline

When an object experiences friction in an incline, the friction experienced is equal to the weight which is perpendicular to the plane where:

$$
n=w\cos\theta\\ \\
\mu_{k}=\frac{f_{k}}{n}=\frac{w\sin\theta_{k}}{w\cos\theta_{k}}=\tan\theta_{k}\\
\mu_{s}=\tan\theta_{s}
$$

# Tension Force

The tension force is the pulling/stretching force acted upon the object attached by a rope/cord/cable.

# Weight/Gravitational Force

The gravitational force is the force acted upon the object by an object with a significantly larger mass (usually the planet where the object resides in).

$$
W=F_{grav}=mg
$$

Where:
${}m={}$mass of the object
${}g={}$acceleration due to gravity

# Superposition of Forces

The superposition of forces is also known as the resultant and is computed by the sum of all forces.

$$
\vec{R}=\vec{F}_{1}+\vec{F}_{2}+\vec{F}_{3}+\dots+\vec{F}_{N}=\Sigma \vec{F}
$$

The resultant can also be computed with ${}x,y,z{}$ components as applicable

$$
R_{x}=\Sigma F_{x}\\
R_{y}=\Sigma F_{y}\\
R_{z}=\Sigma F_{z}\\
R=\sqrt{ R_{x}^{2}+R_{y}^{2}+R_{z}^{2} }
$$
