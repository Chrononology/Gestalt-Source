**Polynomial** is a core concept of [[Algebra]].

Polynomials are algebraic expressions in the form of:

$$
a_{n}x^{n}+a_{n-1}x^{x-1}+\dots+a_{1}x+a_{0}
$$

Operations between polynomials fall under the same fashion as normal algebraic expressions.

# Factorization

There are 4 ways of factoring a polynomial

## Common Factors

We can factor a polynomial by identifying its common factors. For example:

$$
6x^{2}+3x=3x(2x+1)
$$

## Grouping

We can factor a polynomial by grouping together pairs of terms with common factors. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^{3}+8x^{2}+3x+12&=2x^{2}(x+4)+3(x+4) \\
 & =(2x^{2}+3)(x+4)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Splitting

We can factor a polynomial by splitting the middle term (usually ${}b{}$) into its factors then proceed with factor by grouping. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x^{2}+56x+8&=7x^{2}+7x+8x+8 \\
 & =7x(x+1)+8(x+1) \\
 & =(7x+8)(x+1)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Algebraic Identities

We can factor a polynomial by checking if it is a [[Algebra#Special Identities|Special Identity]] and using the Special identities to factor it. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
21x^{3}+135x^{2}+225x+125&=3^{3}x^{3}+(3)(3^{2}x^{2})(5)+(3)(3x)(5^{2})+5^{3} \\
 & =(3x+5)^{3} \\
 & =(3x+5)(3x+5)(3x+5)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Remainder Theorem

The remainder of a polynomial ${}p(x){}$ is divided by ${}x-a{}$ as ${}p(a){}$.

## Factor Theorem

Any polynomial ${}p(x){}$ is divisible by ${}x-a{}$ if and only if ${}p(a)=0{}$.
