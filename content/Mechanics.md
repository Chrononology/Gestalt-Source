# Units and Vectors

_Main notes: [[Units]], [[Vectors]]_
A Scalar quantity is just a quantity or just a magnitude. A Vector quantity involves magnitude **and** direction.

A common example of this is:
Distance and Displacement

They both involve length. However, distance is the total length of the travelled path while displacement is the change in position.
To describe displacement with just how far an object moves is not enough, it also needs direction.

One may travel 20km from a house to a shop and come back but their total displacement is simply ${}0{}$.

# Work and Energy

## Work

Work is the product of the applied force and distance travelled/displacement

$$
W=Fd=Fs=F \Delta s=F \Delta d
$$

Where ${}W={}$work, ${}F={}$Force, ${}d={}$distance
The unit of work is ${}J{}$ or "Joule" /joole/ or /joe-le/

$$
1J=1Nm=1 kg \frac{m^{2}}{s_{2}}
$$

$$
W_{tot}=W_{net}\\ \\
\begin{aligned}
W_{tot}>0\qquad&\text{speed up} \\
W_{tot}<0\qquad&\text{slows down} \\
W_{tot}=0\qquad &\text{maintains the same speed}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Energy

Kinetic energy

$$
T=\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}
$$

Work-Energy Theorem

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{f}^{2}=v_{i}^{2}+2a\Delta x & \implies mv_{f}^{2}=mv_{i}^{2}+2ma\Delta x \\
 & \implies \frac{1}{2}mv_{f}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}mv_{i}^{2}+ma\Delta x \\
 & \implies \frac{1}{2}mv_{f}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}mv_{i}^{2}=ma\Delta x \\
 & \implies KE_{f}-KE_{i}=W
\end{aligned}
$$

Gravitational potential energy

$$
U_{grav}=mgy=mgh\\
W_{grav}=mg(y_{1}-y_{2})=-\Delta U_{grav}
$$

Total mechanical energy

$$
E=T+U=\text{constant}
$$

Conservation of Mechanical energy

$$
-\Delta U=\Delta T\\
\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}_{i}+mgy_{i}=\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}_{f}+mgy_{f}\\
\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}_{i}+mgy_{i}+W_{other}=\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}_{f}+mgy_{f}
$$

Elastic Potential Energy

$$
U_{el}=\frac{1}{2}kx^{2}
$$

Where:
${}k={}$spring stiffness or force constant (${}\frac{N}{m}{}$)

Elastic Work

$$
W_{el}=-\Delta U_{el}
$$

Hooke's Law

$$
F=-kx
$$
