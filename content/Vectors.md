**Vectors** are magnitudes with direction. They are prominently used in [[Physics]] particularly [[Mechanics]].

Vector quantities are represented by having an arrow on top of a letter like: ${}\vec{A}{}$
Vectors are represented as an arrow with a tip and a tail.
Two (or more) vectors are **parallel** if they have the same direction. Meaning vectors of the opposite direction are **anti-parallel**. And are **equal** if they both have the same magnitude and direction.
The magnitude of a vector may be represented by: ${}|\vec{A}|{}$

# Addition and Subtraction of Vectors

The vector sum or resultant is obtained by "adding" both vectors

## Parallel and Anti-Parallel Vectors

IF the vectors are parallel or anti-parallel (with one vector being a negative value) we use:

$$
\vec{C}=\vec{A}+\vec{B}=\vec{B}+\vec{A}
$$

The resultant of multiple parallel or anti-parallel vectors can be obtained by using the very same equation where you can add other vectors like ${}\vec{D}{}$ to the equation.

## Vectors at Right Angles

IF the vectors are at right angles with each other, the resultant can be obtained by using the Pythagorean theorem:

$$
\vec{R}=\sqrt{ \vec{A}^{2}+\vec{B}^{2} }
$$

The direction can be computed by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\theta&=\frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}}\\
\theta&=\tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} \right)
\end{aligned}
$$

# Vector Components

Vectors can be split into its ${}x{}$ and ${}y{}$ components by:

$$
\vec{A_{x}}=\vec{A}\cos\theta\\
\vec{A_{y}}=\vec{A}\sin\theta
$$

Its resultant can be computed by using the Pythagorean theorem:

$$
\vec{R}=\sqrt{ A_{x}^{2}+A_{y}^{2} }
$$

And its direction by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\theta&=\frac{A_{y}}{A_{x}}\\
\theta&=\tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{A_{y}}{A_{x}} \right)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiple Vectors

The resultant of multiple vector components can be resolved by adding up each component of ${}x{}$ and of ${}y{}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum R_{x}&=A_{x}+B_{x}+\dots \\
\sum R_{y} & =A_{y}+B_{y}+\dots
\end{aligned}
$$

Its resultant can be computed by:

$$
\vec{R}=\sqrt{ \Sigma R_{x}^{2}+\Sigma R_{y}^{2} }
$$

And its direction by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\theta&=\frac{\Sigma R_{y}}{\Sigma R_{x}}\\
\theta&=\tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{\Sigma R_{y}}{\Sigma R_{x}} \right)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Vectors in 3D Space

The resultant of a vector and multiple vectors can also be computed by using the Pythagorean Theorem:

$$
\begin{aligned} \\
\vec{R}&=\sqrt{ A_{x}^{2}+A_{y}^{2}+A_{z}^{2} }\\
\vec{R}&=\sqrt{ \Sigma R_{x}^{2}+\Sigma R_{y}^{2}+\Sigma R_{z}^{2} }
\end{aligned}
$$

# Unit Vectors

Unit vectors are vectors with a magnitude${}=1{}$ and have a direction by pointing toward an axis

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{A}_{x}&=A_{x}\hat{i} \\
\vec{A}_{y}&=A_{y}\hat{j} \\
\vec{A}_{z}&=A_{z}\hat{k}
\end{aligned}
\\
\vec{A}=A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k}
$$

The vector sum of unit vectors can be computed by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{A} & =A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j} \\
\vec{B} & =B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j} \\
\vec{R} & =\vec{A}+\vec{B} \\
 & =(A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j})+(B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}) \\
 & =(A_{x}+B_{x})\hat{i}+(A_{y}+B_{y})\hat{j} \\
 & =R_{x}\hat{i}+R_{y}\hat{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

The same is true if the vector is in a 3D space:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{A} & =A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k} \\
\vec{B} & =B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}+B_{z}\hat{k} \\
\vec{R} & =\vec{A}+\vec{B} \\
 & =(A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k})+(B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}+B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & =(A_{x}+B_{x})\hat{i}+(A_{y}+B_{y})\hat{j}+(A_{z}+B_{z})\hat{k} \\
 & =R_{x}\hat{i}+R_{y}\hat{j}+R_{z}\hat{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

The magnitude of multiple unit vectors can also be computed using the Pythagorean Theorem:

$$
|\vec{R}|=\sqrt{ R_{x}^{2}+R_{y}^{2}+R_{z}^{2} }
$$

# Vector Products

## Scalar/Dot Product

The scalar product of 2 vectors is the magnitude of ${}\vec{A}{}$ multiplied by the component of ${}\vec{B}{}$ in the direction of ${}\vec{A}{}$
The result of a scalar product is a scalar quantity as the name suggests

$$
\vec{A}\cdot \vec{B}=AB\cos \phi
$$

Where:
${}\phi={}$the angle between the vectors

### Scalar Product using Components

Note that:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat{i}\cdot \hat{i}&=\hat{j}\cdot \hat{j}=\hat{k}\cdot\hat{k}=(1)(1)\cos 0^{\circ}=1 \\
\hat{i}\cdot \hat{j}&=\hat{i}\cdot \hat{k}=\hat{j}\cdot\hat{k}=(1)(1)\cos 90^{\circ}=0
\end{aligned}
$$

By the vector unit method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{A} & =A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k} \\
\vec{B} & =B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}+B_{z}\hat{k} \\
\vec{A}\cdot \vec{B} & =(A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k})\cdot(B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}+B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & =(A_{x}\hat{i}\cdot\hat{i})+(A_{x}\hat{i}\cdot B_{y}\hat{j})+(A_{x}\hat{i}\cdot B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{y}\hat{j}\cdot B_{x}\hat{i})+(A_{y}\hat{j}\cdot B_{y}\hat{j})+(A_{y}\hat{j}\cdot B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{z}\hat{k}\cdot B_{x}\hat{i})+(A_{z}\hat{k}\cdot B_{y}\hat{j})+(A_{z}\hat{k}\cdot B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & =(A_{x}B_{x}\hat{i}\cdot \hat{i})+(A_{x} B_{y}\hat{i}\cdot\hat{j})+(A_{x} B_{z}\hat{i}\cdot\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{y} B_{x}\hat{j}\cdot\hat{i})+(A_{y} B_{y}\hat{j}\cdot\hat{j})+(A_{y} B_{z}\hat{j}\cdot\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{z} B_{x}\hat{k}\cdot\hat{i})+(A_{z} B_{y}\hat{k}\cdot\hat{j})+(A_{z} B_{z}\hat{k}\cdot\hat{k}) \\
 & =(A_{x}B_{x}\cancelto{ 1 }{ \hat{i}\cdot \hat{i} })+(A_{x} B_{y}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{i}\cdot\hat{j} })+(A_{x} B_{z}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{i}\cdot\hat{k} }) \\
 & \quad+(A_{y} B_{x}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{j}\cdot\hat{i} })+(A_{y} B_{y}\cancelto{ 1 }{ \hat{j}\cdot\hat{j} })+(A_{y} B_{z}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{j}\cdot\hat{k} }) \\
 & \quad+(A_{z} B_{x}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{k}\cdot\hat{i} })+(A_{z} B_{y}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{k}\cdot\hat{j} })+(A_{z} B_{z}\cancelto{ 1 }{ \hat{k}\cdot\hat{k} }) \\
\vec{A}\cdot \vec{B}& =(A_{x}B_{x})+(A_{y} B_{y})+(A_{z} B_{z})
\end{aligned}
$$

### Angle of a Scalar Product

The angle of a scalar product can be computed by:

$$
\cos \phi= \frac{\vec{A}\cdot \vec{B}}{AB}
$$

## Vector/Cross Product

The vector product of 2 vectors lying in a plane is a vector quantity that is points perpendicular to said plane.
The result of a vector product is a vector quantity as the name suggests

$$
A\times B=AB\sin \phi\\
B\times A=BA\sin \phi\\
A\times B\neq B\times A\\
A\times B=A\times -B\\
B\times A=-A\times B
$$

The vector product of two parallel or anti-parallel or any vector with itself is _always_ ${}0{}$

### Vector Product using Components

Note that:

$$
\hat{i}\times \hat{i}=\hat{j}\times \hat{j}=\hat{k}\times \hat{k}=0\\
\hat{i}\times \hat{j}=-\hat{j}\times \hat{i}=\hat{k}\\
\hat{j}\times \hat{k}=-\hat{k}\times \hat{j}=\hat{i}\\
\hat{k}\times \hat{i}=-\hat{i}\times \hat{k}=\hat{j}
$$

By the vector unit method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{A} & =A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k} \\
\vec{B} & =B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}+B_{z}\hat{k} \\
\vec{A}\times \vec{B} & =(A_{x}\hat{i}+A_{y}\hat{j}+A_{z}\hat{k})\times(B_{x}\hat{i}+B_{y}\hat{j}+B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & =(A_{x}\hat{i}\times\hat{i})+(A_{x}\hat{i}\times B_{y}\hat{j})+(A_{x}\hat{i}\times B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{y}\hat{j}\times B_{x}\hat{i})+(A_{y}\hat{j}\times B_{y}\hat{j})+(A_{y}\hat{j}\times B_{z}\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{z}\hat{k}\times B_{x}\hat{i})+(A_{z}\hat{k}\times B_{y}\hat{j})+(A_{z}\hat{k}\times B_{z}\hat{k}) \\ \\
 & =(A_{x}B_{x}\hat{i}\times \hat{i})+(A_{x} B_{y}\hat{i}\times\hat{j})+(A_{x} B_{z}\hat{i}\times\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{y} B_{x}\hat{j}\times\hat{i})+(A_{y} B_{y}\hat{j}\times\hat{j})+(A_{y} B_{z}\hat{j}\times\hat{k}) \\
 & \quad+(A_{z} B_{x}\hat{k}\times\hat{i})+(A_{z} B_{y}\hat{k}\times\hat{j})+(A_{z} B_{z}\hat{k}\times\hat{k}) \\ \\
 & =(A_{x}B_{x}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{i}\times \hat{i} })+(A_{x} B_{y}\cancelto{ \hat{k} }{ \hat{i}\times\hat{j} })+(A_{x} B_{z}\cancelto{ -\hat{j} }{ \hat{i}\times\hat{k} }) \\
 & \quad+(A_{y} B_{x}\cancelto{ -\hat{k} }{ \hat{j}\times\hat{i} })+(A_{y} B_{y}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{j}\times\hat{j} })+(A_{y} B_{z}\cancelto{ \hat{i} }{ \hat{j}\times\hat{k} }) \\
 & \quad+(A_{z} B_{x}\cancelto{ \hat{j} }{ \hat{k}\times\hat{i} })+(A_{z} B_{y}\cancelto{ -\hat{i} }{ \hat{k}\times\hat{j} })+(A_{z} B_{z}\cancelto{ 0 }{ \hat{k}\times\hat{k} }) \\ \\
 & =(A_{x} B_{y} )\hat{k}-(A_{x} B_{z})\hat{j}-(A_{y} B_{x})\hat{k} \\
 & \quad+(A_{y} B_{z})\hat{i}+(A_{z} B_{x})\hat{j}-(A_{z} B_{y})\hat{i} \\ \\
\vec{A}\times \vec{B} & =(A_{y} B_{z}-A_{z} B_{y})\hat{i}+(A_{z} B_{x}-A_{x} B_{z})\hat{j}+(A_{x} B_{y}-A_{y} B_{x})\hat{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

It can also be done with the determinant form:

$$
\vec{A}\times \vec{B}=
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k} \\
A_{x}&A_{y} & A_{z} \\
B_{x} & B_{y} & B_{z}
\end{vmatrix}
\\
\vec{A}\times \vec{B}=
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k} \\
A_{x}&A_{y} & A_{z} \\
B_{x} & B_{y} & B_{z}
\end{vmatrix}
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat{i}&\hat{j}\\
A_{x}&A_{y} \\
B_{x} & B_{y}
\end{vmatrix}
$$

Where:
The components of ${}\hat{i}{}$ can be computed by subtracting lower right diagonal of the first ${}\hat{i}{}$ (${}A_{y}B_{z}{}$) by the lower left diagonal of the second ${}\hat{i}{}$ (${}A_{z}B_{y}{}$)

The components of ${}\hat{j}{}$ can be computed by subtracting the lower right diagonal of the first ${}\hat{j}{}$ by the lower left diagonal of the second ${}\hat{j}{}$

The components of ${}\hat{k}{}$ can be computed by subtracting the lower right diagonal of ${}\hat{k}{}$ by the lower left diagonal of ${}\hat{k}{}$
